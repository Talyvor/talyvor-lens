# Company jets beta — legitimate-interest assessment (DRAFT, for Nick's approval)

Status: draft written with B52.30, beside the impact assessment (company-jets-dpia.md). The beta stays off until both
are approved.

## Purpose test

Talyvor's interest: research reports on listed companies built from public, licensed data. Company aviation activity
(how often a company's own jets fly, and where they land, a day or more later) is a recognised research signal, and
publishing it with its method and sources serves readers of those reports. The interest is commercial and lawful.

## Necessity test

- Only company-owned jets, only counts per company and day, only days that ended at least 24 hours before.
- No aircraft is ever named in a figure; no positions or routes are kept.
- A less intrusive route that gives the same signal (the company's own disclosures) does not exist in time to matter.

## Balancing test

- Reasonable expectations: company aircraft movements are broadcast publicly (ADS-B) and reported by flight trackers;
  owners who object can use LADD and PIA in the US, which Scan honours.
- Impact on people: limited by excluding individuals, trusts, withheld registrations, LADD and PIA aircraft and
  aircraft used by one person, by the delay, and by aggregation.
- Safeguards: erasure and objection requests take effect at once and delete every stored figure the aircraft was part
  of; the beta is off by default and needs Nick's switch.
- UK and EU aircraft: no LADD or PIA equivalent exists, so GDPR alone applies; the beta reads only the US (FAA)
  registry.

## Conclusion (draft)

The interest is legitimate, the processing is limited to what it needs, and with the exclusions above it does not
override the rights of the people behind the aircraft. To be confirmed by Nick before the switch goes on.
